# Fluia — Company Brief for Outreach Agent

This document gives an outreach agent the facts it needs to write and qualify outreach for Fluia. It does not contain outreach copy. It contains the facts the copy must stay true to.

## 1. What Fluia is

Fluia is an AI implementation consultancy for small and medium businesses (SMEs). Fluia finds where AI creates real value inside a business, then builds and runs the system that captures that value. Fluia does not sell software licenses or off-the-shelf tools. Fluia designs, builds, and operates custom AI systems for each client.

Fluia was founded by two Bocconi University graduates, Jeremy Lorge and Theo Bockenmeyer. Both worked in finance and accounting roles in Paris before founding the company. They saw skilled people lose hours each week to manual, repetitive work, and started Fluia to fix that problem with AI. Both founders use AI tools daily and build every solution in-house.

## 2. The three services

1. **AI Implementation and Integration Review (AIIR).** A one-week, fixed-fee audit of the client's business. Fluia maps the client's processes and identifies where AI adds real value.
2. **Custom web applications.** Fluia builds platforms such as CRMs, portals, and internal tools, tailored to the client's business.
3. **Workflow automation and AI agents.** Fluia builds agents that do specific jobs, such as chasing invoices, screening leads, or drafting outreach.

The AIIR is the entry point for every new client. It is not a loss leader. The fixed fee covers the review and the four weeks that follow.

## 3. How an engagement runs

- **Week 1 — The Review (AIIR).** Fluia interviews stakeholders and gets access to the client's systems. At the end of the week, Fluia delivers a written report with three parts:
  1. The opportunities found, described in plain terms, with the time, cost, or errors each one removes.
  2. An effort-versus-ROI matrix that ranks each opportunity.
  3. A phased roadmap for acting on them.
- **Weeks 2 to 4 — The Build.** Fluia builds the highest-ranked solution from the roadmap. This build is included in the AIIR fee. The client pays no extra cost during this phase.
- **End of Week 4 — The Decision.** Fluia and the client review what has been built. The client picks one of four options:
  - **Continue** (recommended route): a revenue share or a monthly retainer, matched to the type of solution.
  - **Buy out**: a one-time payment that transfers the solution to the client, with no further involvement from Fluia.
  - **New build**: move to the next opportunity on the roadmap. This mini-review restarts the same cycle.
  - **End**: the client walks away. Nothing further is owed beyond the original AIIR fee.

This structure keeps the client's commitment low until they have seen working software.

## 4. Pricing

- **AIIR:** a fixed fee of €3,000. This is the only cost until the client decides to continue.
- **Financial-performance solutions** (e.g., invoice chasing, lead screening, collections): after week 4, a 20 percent share of the new revenue the solution directly generates, billed every four weeks for an agreed period. Maintenance and updates are included.
- **Operational solutions** (e.g., internal CRMs, knowledge tools, document workflows): after week 4, a monthly retainer. It covers maintenance, usage costs, iteration, support, and the cost savings delivered.
- **Buyout:** a one-time fee. Fluia hands over the solution and takes on no further work.

Use these figures only when a prospect asks for pricing directly, or when the outreach explicitly promises a cost. Do not lead outreach messages with price.

## 5. Who Fluia serves (the ideal customer profile)

Fluia's target business:

- Has **5 to 100 employees**.
- Runs on **systems that do not talk to each other** (e.g., separate tools for invoicing, CRM, and operations, with manual re-entry between them).
- Has **work that repeats in the same shape every week** (the same report, the same follow-up email, the same data entry).
- Holds **years of data that nobody reads or uses**.
- Has **leadership that is fairly confident AI can help**, but does not know which part of the business to start with, or what it would take.

This is a traits-based profile, not a sector-based one. It fits accounting firms, agencies, associations, and other SMEs equally, as long as the traits above hold true. Disconnected systems is the strongest single signal that a business fits.

## 6. Proof points (what Fluia has built)

- **AR Agent.** Reads a client's accounts receivable ledger every Monday, scores each invoice by urgency, and drafts a reminder email. Connects QuickBooks Online with HubSpot, Outlook, and Teams. A person reviews low-confidence cases before anything sends.
- **UniAssoc.** A custom, multi-tenant CRM platform for student associations, covering application tracking, interviews, members, and finance in one system. Built in about two weeks.
- **Lead Finder.** Scans the web against a client's ideal customer profile, scores each match, and delivers a ranked shortlist of leads.
- **Lead reactivation agent.** Segments dormant CRM contacts and drafts personalized reactivation emails automatically.
- **NATO procurement agent.** Scans defense procurement listings overnight and scores each opportunity against a client profile.
- **LinkedIn monitoring automation.** Tracks activity on selected LinkedIn pages and extracts named entities for a client.

Use these as concrete examples in outreach. Do not invent additional case studies or metrics.

## 7. Positioning and voice

- Fluia positions itself as an honest, hands-on alternative to AI tool vendors and large consultancies. The founders build every solution themselves. Fluia does not resell software.
- Lead with the review, not the sale. The AIIR gives the prospect a concrete, written plan even if they choose not to continue with Fluia.
- Speak in plain, first-person terms ("we build," "we found"). Avoid vague claims. Use specific, concrete proof (the examples in Section 6) instead of general promises.
- Do not use invented statistics or metrics that are not listed in this brief.
- Avoid marketing language such as "seamless," "cutting-edge," or "revolutionary." State what the system does and what it removes (time, cost, or errors).
- Qualifying signal to listen for in a prospect's reply: mentions of manual data entry, tools that do not sync, or a backlog of repetitive work. These map directly to the ICP in Section 5.
